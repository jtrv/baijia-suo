Mixed-size outputs reset the shared animation every frame

bug both reviews external: HIGH

src/wayland.rs:193-244 · src/app.rs:131-135 · player.rs ensure_sized

Problem: one player, per-output ensure_sized() — two differently sized monitors reset the animation and reallocate its canvas on every draw.

Resolution: per-output players (matches xlockmore's per-screen state model); same-sized outputs may share a frame.

  • invisible on single-monitor T480s
  • needs a multi-resolution regression test

clears_each_frame() is an insufficient boolean

Problem: one flag conflates three render modes; ~21 canvas-copying modes are treated as tick/render-paired, so catch-up copies full intermediate canvases that can never be presented.

Resolution: a three-state RenderPolicy — Incremental · ClearThenRender · CompleteFrame — defaulting to Incremental, modes opted in after audit.

Lightning invents a ~1s black pause upstream doesn't have

bug — verified explains user report corrects inline review

lightning.c:593-598 · src/animation/modes/lightning.rs:517-523

Problem: upstream's stage 4 calls init_lightning() immediately every invocation (its busyLoop is vestigial); the port waits busy_loop > 100 — an extra second of black between storms.

Resolution: reinitialize immediately in stage 4; this is the remaining "lightning feels slow".

  • inline review wrongly called the pause faithful
  • smallest fix, largest user-visible payoff

DoublePool is not actually bounded to two buffers

act on it external only

src/render/pool.rs:138-183

Problem: when no free buffer matches, get_buffer() allocates another memfd — frame callbacks can outrun wl_buffer releases, so the pool can grow (33 MB per 4K buffer).

Resolution: cap at 2–3 buffers; defer presentation when all busy, redraw on the release event.

Verdict & merged order

The reviews agree on the architecture-level frontier (output ownership, RenderPolicy, copy chain, measurement first). review.txt additionally caught two verified lightning fidelity bugs — one being the reported slowness — and an MSRV break introduced by recent work. Both reviews independently rejected a GPU renderer.
